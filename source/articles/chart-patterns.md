# Chart patterns: double tops and bottoms, head and shoulders, flags

**Covers:** double top, double bottom, head and shoulders, flag pattern

## Shapes that repeat {#overview}
Traders have noticed for a long time that price charts often form recognizable shapes. The reason is simple: many traders watch the same levels and react in the same way, so their combined behavior leaves similar footprints. Patterns are not laws. They are situations where the odds may be slightly better, if the pattern is confirmed.

## Double top {#double-top}
A double top is a pattern in which the stock rallies to about the same high price twice, with a dip in between. The shape looks like the letter M. It shows that buyers failed twice to push the price above that level, and it often signals a reversal downward. It is confirmed when the price falls below the low point of the dip in the middle.

## Double bottom {#double-bottom}
A double bottom is the mirror image: the stock falls to about the same low twice, with a bounce in between. It looks like a W. It shows that sellers failed twice, and it often signals a reversal upward. It is confirmed when the price rises above the high of the bounce in between.

## Head and shoulders {#head-and-shoulders}
Head and shoulders is a pattern with three peaks: a higher middle peak, the head, flanked by two lower ones, the shoulders. A line joining the lows between the peaks is the neckline. It often signals that an uptrend is about to reverse. The pattern is confirmed when the price falls below the neckline.

## Flag pattern {#flag-pattern}
A flag pattern is a pause in a strong move. After a sharp rise or fall, the price drifts sideways or slightly against the trend, inside a small rectangle shaped like a flag on a pole. It often leads to a continuation of the original trend. The sharp move is the pole, and the small rectangle is the flag.

## A worked example {#example}
**Double top.** A stock rises from 40 to 50, falls back to 46, and rises again to 50.1. It fails there, and the price turns down. The dip low in the middle is 46.

- Pattern height: the peak minus the dip low = 50 − 46 = 4.
- Confirmation: the price closes below 46.
- A common rough target: the confirmation level minus the height = 46 − 4 = 42.

A trader might sell short at 45.8, with a protective exit at 50.3, just above the double top. The risk per share is 50.3 − 45.8 = 4.5. The target 42 would earn 45.8 − 42 = 3.8. The reward is lower than the risk, so this particular plan is not attractive, and a careful trader might skip it or enter closer to the top.

**Head and shoulders.** The left shoulder peaks at 30, the head at 34 and the right shoulder at 30.5. The lows between them are near 28. That line, at 28, is the neckline. The head's height above the neckline is 34 − 28 = 6. If the price closes below 28, the rough target is 28 − 6 = 22.

**Flag.** A stock jumps from 20 to 24 in two days: a pole of 4. It then drifts between 23.5 and 24 for three days, on shrinking activity. The trader waits for a close above 24. A rough target is the flag's breakout point plus the pole: 24 + 4 = 28.

| Pattern | Shape | Usual meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Double top | M | Reversal down |
| Double bottom | W | Reversal up |
| Head and shoulders | Three peaks | Reversal of an uptrend |
| Flag | Small rectangle after a sharp move | Trend continues |

The numbers above are rough rules of thumb. They fail often. Treat a pattern as a reason to look, and use confirmation and a protective exit.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Seeing patterns everywhere. Almost any chart can be made to look like a head and shoulders if you want it to. Require clear shapes, confirmation and a plan for being wrong. Another mistake is entering before the confirmation, only because the shape looks promising.

## Key points {#key-points}
- Double tops (M) and double bottoms (W) suggest reversals once the middle level breaks.
- Head and shoulders has three peaks and a neckline. It suggests a reversal.
- A flag is a small pause in a strong move, and it suggests continuation.
- Wait for confirmation and always know where you will leave if wrong.
