# Chart patterns: double tops and bottoms, head and shoulders, flags

**Covers:** double top, double bottom, head and shoulders, flag pattern

## Shapes that repeat {#overview}
If you look at enough price charts, you start to notice that certain shapes come back again and again. There is a reason. Many traders watch the same price levels and react in the same way, so their combined behavior leaves similar footprints. A pattern is not a law, though. It describes a situation where the odds may lean slightly to one side, and only if the pattern is confirmed. Let's meet four of them.

## Double top {#double-top}
A double top forms when a stock rises to about the same high twice, with a dip in between. Drawn on the chart, it looks like the letter M. The message is that buyers tried twice to push through that level and failed both times, so a reversal downward becomes more likely. The pattern counts as confirmed when the price falls below the low of the dip in the middle.

## Double bottom {#double-bottom}
A double bottom is the mirror image. The stock falls to about the same low twice, with a bounce between the two falls, and the shape looks like a W. Sellers failed twice, so a reversal upward becomes more likely. Confirmation comes when the price rises above the top of the bounce in the middle.

## Head and shoulders {#head-and-shoulders}
This pattern has three peaks. The middle one is the highest and is called the head. The two lower peaks on either side are the shoulders. Draw a line through the lows between the peaks, and you have the neckline. The pattern often appears at the end of an uptrend, and it is confirmed when the price falls below the neckline.

## Flag pattern {#flag-pattern}
A flag is a pause in the middle of a strong move. After a sharp rise or fall, the price drifts sideways, or slightly against the move, inside a small rectangle. The sharp move is the pole, and the rectangle is the flag. Traders often expect the original move to continue once the flag ends.

## A worked example {#example}
**Double top.** A stock rises from 40 to 50, falls back to 46, then rises again to 50.1 and fails. The price turns down. The low of the middle dip is 46.

- The pattern's height is the peak minus the dip low: 50 − 46 = 4.
- Confirmation comes when the price closes below 46.
- A rough target is the confirmation level minus the height: 46 − 4 = 42.

Suppose a trader sells short at 45.8, with a protective exit at 50.3, right above the double top. The risk per share is 50.3 − 45.8 = 4.5. The target of 42 would earn 45.8 − 42 = 3.8. The reward is smaller than the risk, so this plan is not attractive as it stands. A careful trader would pass on it, or look for an entry closer to the top.

**Head and shoulders.** The left shoulder peaks at 30, the head at 34 and the right shoulder at 30.5. The lows between the peaks are near 28, so the neckline sits at 28. The head rises 34 − 28 = 6 above the neckline. If the price closes below 28, the rough target is 28 − 6 = 22.

**Flag.** A stock jumps from 20 to 24 in two days, so the pole is 4. Then it drifts between 23.5 and 24 for three days while activity shrinks. The trader waits for a close above 24. A rough target is the breakout point plus the pole: 24 + 4 = 28.

| Pattern | Shape | Usual meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Double top | M | Reversal down |
| Double bottom | W | Reversal up |
| Head and shoulders | Three peaks | Reversal of an uptrend |
| Flag | Small rectangle after a sharp move | Trend continues |

These targets are rough rules of thumb, and they fail often. Treat a pattern as a reason to take a closer look, and pair it with confirmation and a protective exit.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
A common slip is to see patterns everywhere. If you want a head and shoulders badly enough, almost any chart will supply one. Ask for a shape that is distinct, wait for the confirmation, and have a plan for the case where you are wrong. The other slip is entering before the confirmation because the shape looks promising.

## Key points {#key-points}
- Double tops (M) and double bottoms (W) suggest a reversal once the middle level breaks.
- Head and shoulders has three peaks and a neckline, and it suggests a reversal.
- A flag is a small pause inside a strong move, and it suggests the move continues.
- Wait for confirmation, and know in advance where you will leave if you are wrong.
