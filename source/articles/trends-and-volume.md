# Trends, trendlines and trading volume

**Covers:** uptrend, downtrend, trendline, range-bound, volume, volume spike

## Direction and participation {#overview}
Two questions come before almost every trade: where is the price heading, and how many people are behind the move? Trends answer the first. Volume answers the second.

## Uptrend {#uptrend}
An uptrend is a stretch of time in which the price is making higher highs and higher lows, moving upward overall. Each rally goes a bit further than the last, and each dip stops a bit higher than the last. The price can still fall on many days inside it. What matters is the sequence of peaks and troughs.

## Downtrend {#downtrend}
A downtrend is the mirror image: lower highs and lower lows, moving downward overall. Each bounce is weaker than the one before, and each fall goes further.

## Range-bound {#range-bound}
A range-bound stock moves sideways between a fairly steady floor and ceiling, without a clear direction up or down. The highs are about equal and so are the lows. Many stocks spend more time in ranges than in trends, and a strategy built for trends does poorly in a range.

## Trendline {#trendline}
A trendline is a straight line drawn across a chart, joining a stock's recent lows in an uptrend, or its recent highs in a downtrend, to show the direction it has been moving. Two points define the line, and a third touch makes it credible. When the price breaks through a trendline, the trend may be ending.

## Volume {#volume}
Volume is the number of shares that actually changed hands, bought and sold, during a given period. It is usually drawn as bars below the chart. A price move on high volume has many participants behind it. The same move on low volume could be the work of a few orders.

## Volume spike {#volume-spike}
A volume spike is a sudden burst of trading, much larger than usual. It often signals unusually strong interest, caused by news, an earnings report or a break through an important level. A spike on a move up shows strong buying. A spike on a move down shows strong selling.

## A worked example {#example}
Look at the daily lows and highs of a stock over six days:

| Day | High | Low |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 50.0 | 48.0 |
| 2 | 51.0 | 49.0 |
| 3 | 50.5 | 49.5 |
| 4 | 52.0 | 50.0 |
| 5 | 51.5 | 50.5 |
| 6 | 53.0 | 51.0 |

The highs go 50.0, 51.0, 52.0, 53.0 on days 1, 2, 4, 6, always rising, and the lows 48.0, 49.0, 50.0, 51.0 rise as well. This is an uptrend. Draw a line through the lows of days 1, 2, 4 and 6. It climbs by about 1.0 every two days. That is the trendline. A trader might say: "As long as the price stays above this line, I look for dips to buy. If a candle closes well below it, I stop."

Now add volume. The average daily volume of this stock is 1,000,000 shares. On day 6, it trades 3,500,000. That is 3.5 times the average, a volume spike. The new high on day 6 came with strong participation, which makes it more believable. If the same new high had come with 400,000 shares, 40% of normal, a cautious trader would doubt it.

A range, for contrast:

| Day | High | Low |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 50.0 | 48.0 |
| 2 | 50.1 | 48.1 |
| 3 | 49.9 | 47.9 |
| 4 | 50.0 | 48.0 |

The highs hover near 50 and the lows near 48. Nothing is rising or falling. This stock is range-bound, with a band 2.0 wide. A buy near 48 and a sell near 50 could earn 2.0 per share, but a break out of the band ends the pattern.

| Situation | Typical approach |
|---|---|
| Uptrend | Look for dips to buy, stay above the trendline |
| Downtrend | Avoid buying, or look for chances to sell short |
| Range-bound | Trade between the edges, watch for a break |
| Volume spike | Check the news and whether the move holds |

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Drawing a trendline to fit a view that one already has, by choosing only the points that fit. A line needs at least three honest touches. Another mistake is ignoring volume, and trusting a price move that almost nobody traded.

## Key points {#key-points}
- An uptrend makes higher highs and higher lows. A downtrend makes lower highs and lower lows. A range moves sideways.
- A trendline joins lows (uptrend) or highs (downtrend).
- Volume measures participation. A volume spike signals unusual interest.
- A move with strong volume is more trustworthy than one without.
