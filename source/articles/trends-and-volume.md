# Trends, trendlines and trading volume

**Covers:** uptrend, downtrend, trendline, range-bound, volume, volume spike

## Direction and participation {#overview}
Two questions come before almost every trade. Where is the price heading? And how many people are behind the move? Trends answer the first question, and volume answers the second.

## Uptrend {#uptrend}
An uptrend is a stretch of time in which the price makes higher highs and higher lows, so it moves upward overall. Each rally goes a little further than the last, and each dip stops a little higher than the last. The price can still fall on many days inside an uptrend. What matters is the sequence of peaks and dips.

## Downtrend {#downtrend}
A downtrend is the mirror image: lower highs and lower lows, moving downward overall. Each bounce is weaker than the one before, and each fall goes further.

## Range-bound {#range-bound}
A range-bound stock moves sideways between a fairly steady floor and a fairly steady ceiling, with no clear direction. The highs are about equal and so are the lows. Many stocks spend more time in ranges than in trends, and a strategy built for trends does poorly in a range.

## Trendline {#trendline}
A trendline is a straight line drawn across the chart. In an uptrend it joins the recent lows, and in a downtrend it joins the recent highs, to show the direction the stock has been moving. Two points define the line, and a third touch makes it credible. When the price breaks through a trendline, the trend may be ending.

## Volume {#volume}
Volume is the number of shares that actually changed hands, bought and sold, during a period. It is usually drawn as bars below the chart. A price move on high volume has many participants behind it. The same move on low volume could be the work of a few orders.

## Volume spike {#volume-spike}
A volume spike is a sudden burst of trading, much larger than usual. It often points to unusually strong interest, caused by news, an earnings report or a break through an important level. A spike during a move up shows strong buying, and a spike during a move down shows strong selling.

## A worked example {#example}
Here are the daily highs and lows of a stock over six days:

| Day | High | Low |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 50.0 | 48.0 |
| 2 | 51.0 | 49.0 |
| 3 | 50.5 | 49.5 |
| 4 | 52.0 | 50.0 |
| 5 | 51.5 | 50.5 |
| 6 | 53.0 | 51.0 |

Let's read it. The highs of the up days (1, 2, 4 and 6) go 50.0, 51.0, 52.0, 53.0, always rising. Days 3 and 5 are small dips, and their lows (49.5 and 50.5) still sit above the lows before them. The lows rise on every day: 48.0, 49.0, 49.5, 50.0, 50.5, 51.0. This is an uptrend.

Now draw a line through the lows. They climb from 48.0 to 51.0 in five days, roughly 0.6 per day, and that line is the trendline. A trader might say: "As long as the price stays above this line, I look for dips to buy. If a candle closes well below it, I stop."

Now add volume. The stock's average daily volume is 1,000,000 shares. On day 6 it trades 3,500,000, which is 3.5 times the average, a volume spike. The new high on day 6 came with strong participation, which makes it more believable. If the same new high had come with 400,000 shares, 40% of normal, a cautious trader would doubt it.

For contrast, here is a range:

| Day | High | Low |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 50.0 | 48.0 |
| 2 | 50.1 | 48.1 |
| 3 | 49.9 | 47.9 |
| 4 | 50.0 | 48.0 |

The highs hover near 50 and the lows near 48. Nothing is rising or falling, so this stock is range-bound, in a band 2.0 wide. Buying near 48 and selling near 50 could earn 2.0 per share, but a break out of the band ends the pattern.

| Situation | Typical approach |
|---|---|
| Uptrend | Look for dips to buy, stay above the trendline |
| Downtrend | Avoid buying, or look for chances to sell short |
| Range-bound | Trade between the edges, watch for a break |
| Volume spike | Check the news and whether the move holds |

## A common mistake {#mistake}
A common slip is to draw a trendline that fits the view you already hold, by choosing only the points that suit it. An honest line needs at least three touches. Another slip is to ignore volume and trust a price move that almost nobody traded.

## Key points {#key-points}
- An uptrend makes higher highs and higher lows. A downtrend makes lower highs and lower lows. A range moves sideways.
- A trendline joins lows (uptrend) or highs (downtrend).
- Volume measures participation. A volume spike signals unusual interest.
- A move with strong volume is more trustworthy than one without.
