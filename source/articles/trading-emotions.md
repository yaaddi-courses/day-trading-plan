# Trading psychology: FOMO, revenge trading, tilt and overtrading

**Covers:** FOMO, revenge trading, tilt, overtrading

## The market tests your emotions {#overview}
Most of the money lost by beginners is not lost by wrong analysis, but by emotional decisions. Prices move fast, money is at stake, and the mind reacts to both. Knowing the common patterns helps you notice them early, when a break is still possible.

## FOMO {#fomo}
FOMO, fear of missing out, is the anxious urge to jump into a trade late, because a stock is moving fast and you do not want to miss the gain. The price has already risen, so you enter at a worse level, with a stop far away. The move often ends right after you join.

## Revenge trading {#revenge-trading}
Revenge trading means immediately making a new, often oversized trade to win back money just lost, instead of following your normal plan. The motive is to erase the loss, not to follow a setup. It turns one lost trade into several.

## Tilt {#tilt}
Tilt is the angry, frustrated state that can follow a loss, and that makes a trader act on impulse and drop the rules. The word comes from poker. Signs include raised heart rate, anger at the screen, breaking your own rules and a feeling that the market is personally against you.

## Overtrading {#overtrading}
Overtrading means placing far more trades than your plan calls for, often driven by boredom, excitement or the urge to force profits. Each extra trade adds costs, and most of the extra trades are poor ones.

## A worked example {#example}
A trader's plan allows at most 4 trades a day, each risking 100 dollars.

**Morning.** Trade 1 loses 100. Trade 2 loses 100. The account is down 200, and the trader is annoyed. This is the start of tilt.

**Revenge.** He sees a stock moving up fast, and takes a trade of three times the usual size, with a stop twice as wide, to "get it back in one go". The risk is 3 × 2 × 100 = 600 dollars, six times his rule. The trade loses. Total for the morning: −800.

**FOMO.** A different stock jumps 6% in ten minutes. He buys after the jump, afraid to miss the rest. By the time he enters, most of the move is done. The price falls back, and he loses another 150.

**Overtrading.** Bored and wishing to "make the day green", he makes 9 more small trades. With a cost of 3 dollars per trade in spreads and fees, that is 27 dollars on costs alone, and without a setup each trade is a coin flip that costs money. By the close, the day shows −1,100 dollars: 950 from the first four trades and 150 from the small ones, costs included.

Compare to the plan. A rule that size never rises after a loss would have capped the revenge trade at 100 dollars of risk, and a daily loss limit of 300 would have ended the day right after it. The damage would have been about 300 dollars, not 1,100. The difference of 800 dollars, which is 4% of a 20,000 dollar account, came from the emotional decisions that no rule stopped.

| Pattern | Trigger | Counter |
|---|---|---|
| FOMO | A fast move you missed | Wait for the next setup; there is always one |
| Revenge trading | A loss | Stop for 15 minutes; keep the normal size |
| Tilt | A series of losses | Stop trading for the day |
| Overtrading | Boredom, excitement | A hard maximum number of trades |

Practical tools: a written daily limit on trades and losses, a short break after every losing trade, a rule that size never rises after a loss, and a journal where you write what you felt before each trade.

Notice the signs in your body. If your hands are shaking, your jaw is tight, or you are talking to the screen, you are not in a state to make decisions. Closing the platform is the best trade of the day.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Believing "I will not do that" without a rule in place. In the moment, a person on tilt feels perfectly rational. Make the rules in advance, in writing, and make them automatic where you can. Another mistake is treating emotions as a weakness to hide. They are normal, and the aim is to catch them.

## Key points {#key-points}
- FOMO is chasing a move after it has run. Revenge trading is trying to win back a loss at once.
- Tilt is frustration that breaks discipline. Overtrading is too many trades.
- Fixed rules on size, loss and trade count work better than willpower.
- Stopping is a valid trade.
