# Trading psychology: FOMO, revenge trading, tilt and overtrading

**Covers:** FOMO, revenge trading, tilt, overtrading

## The market tests your emotions {#overview}
Most of the money beginners lose is not lost through wrong analysis. It is lost through emotional decisions. Prices move fast, money is at stake, and the mind reacts to both. If you know the common patterns, you can notice them early, while you still have a chance to stop.

## FOMO {#fomo}
FOMO means fear of missing out. A stock is moving fast, and you feel an anxious urge to jump in late so you do not miss the gain. By then the price has already risen, so you enter at a worse level with a stop far away. The move often ends soon after you join.

## Revenge trading {#revenge-trading}
Revenge trading is making a new trade right after a loss, often an oversized one, to win the money back instead of following your normal plan. The motive is to erase the loss, not to follow a setup. It turns one lost trade into several.

## Tilt {#tilt}
Tilt is the angry, frustrated state that can follow a loss. It pushes a trader to act on impulse and drop the rules. The word comes from poker. Typical signs are a racing heart, anger at the screen, breaking your own rules, and a feeling that the market is personally against you.

## Overtrading {#overtrading}
Overtrading means placing far more trades than your plan calls for, often out of boredom, excitement or the urge to force a profit. Every extra trade adds costs, and most of the extra trades are poor ones.

## A worked example {#example}
Imagine a trader whose plan allows at most 4 trades a day, each risking 100 dollars. Let's follow one bad morning.

**Morning.** Trade 1 loses 100. Trade 2 loses 100. The account is down 200, and the trader is annoyed. This is the start of tilt.

**Revenge.** The trader sees a stock moving up fast and takes a trade of three times the usual size, with a stop twice as wide, to "get it back in one go". The risk is 3 × 2 × 100 = 600 dollars, six times the rule. The trade loses. The total for the morning is −800.

**FOMO.** A different stock jumps 6% in ten minutes. The trader buys after the jump, afraid to miss the rest. Most of the move is already over by then. The price falls back, and another 150 is lost.

**Overtrading.** Bored, and wishing to "make the day green", the trader makes 9 more small trades. At 3 dollars per trade in spreads and fees, that is 27 dollars in costs alone. With no setup behind them, each trade is a coin flip that costs money. By the close, the day shows −1,100 dollars: 950 from the first four trades (200 + 600 + 150) and 150 from the small ones, costs included.

Compare that with the plan. A rule that size never rises after a loss would have capped the revenge trade at 100 dollars of risk, and a daily loss limit of 300 dollars would have ended the day right after it. The damage would have been 200 + 100 = 300 dollars, not 1,100. The difference is 800 dollars, which is 4% of a 20,000 dollar account, and it came from emotional decisions that no rule stopped.

| Pattern | Trigger | Counter |
|---|---|---|
| FOMO | A fast move you missed | Wait for the next setup; there is always one |
| Revenge trading | A loss | Stop for 15 minutes; keep the normal size |
| Tilt | A series of losses | Stop trading for the day |
| Overtrading | Boredom, excitement | A hard maximum number of trades |

Some practical tools: a written daily limit on trades and losses, a short break after every losing trade, a rule that size never rises after a loss, and a journal where you write what you felt before each trade.

Learn to notice the signs in your body. If your hands are shaking, your jaw is tight, or you are talking to the screen, you are not in a state to make decisions. Closing the platform is then the best trade of the day.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
A common slip is to believe "I will not do that" without a rule in place. In the moment, a person on tilt feels perfectly rational. Write the rules in advance, and make them automatic where you can. Another slip is to treat emotions as a weakness to hide. They are normal, and the aim is to catch them.

## Key points {#key-points}
- FOMO is chasing a move after it has run. Revenge trading is trying to win back a loss at once.
- Tilt is frustration that breaks discipline. Overtrading is placing too many trades.
- Fixed rules on size, loss and number of trades work better than willpower.
- Stopping is a valid trade.
