# Trading platforms and their main tools

**Covers:** trading platform, trading-platform, order entry, watchlist, charting platform, hotkey

## The software between you and the market {#overview}
Everything a trader does, from watching prices to sending orders, happens through software. Choosing that software and learning it well is a real part of the job. A tool that is slow, or that you do not understand, can cost you more than any bad idea.

## Trading platform {#trading-platform}
A trading platform is the software you use to look at charts, place orders and manage your account. It may be your broker's own app, or a separate program that connects to the broker. Platforms range from simple phone apps to desktop programs with many windows.

The hyphenated form, trading-platform, is the same term placed before another noun, as in "a trading-platform feature".

## Order entry {#order-entry}
Order entry is the part of the platform where you build and send a buy or sell order, by typing or clicking. It usually has fields for the stock symbol, the number of shares, the order type and the price. A good order entry window shows exactly what will be sent before you confirm. Many costly errors happen here, such as an extra zero in the share count.

## Watchlist {#watchlist}
A watchlist is a saved list of stocks you follow closely, even though you do not hold them yet. It shows their prices and changes in one view. Traders usually build a watchlist before the market opens, a short list of stocks that interest them today, so they can focus instead of searching in a hurry.

## Charting platform {#charting-platform}
A charting platform is software built mainly to display and analyze price charts. It may be separate from the platform where you place orders. It offers more drawing tools, more kinds of indicators and the ability to look back through history. Some traders use one program for charts and another for orders, though many platforms combine the two.

## Hotkey {#hotkey}
A hotkey is a keyboard shortcut that you set up in advance so that one key press sends a specific order. Desktop platforms offer it so that a trade does not need several clicks. For example, one key could sell your entire position at the current price. That is fast, and fast can be dangerous, because a mistaken key press sends a real order.

## A worked example {#example}
Let's follow a morning routine, step by step.

1. **Watchlist.** Before the open, you build a list of five stocks that had news overnight. Instead of searching through thousands, you watch five.
2. **Charts.** You open the charting platform and look at each of the five. Two look interesting and three do not, so the list is now two.
3. **Order entry.** When one reaches your planned level, you open the order entry window. You type the symbol, 100 shares and a limit price. Then you check the screen: symbol correct, quantity 100 and not 1,000, price as planned. Only then do you confirm.
4. **Hotkey.** You have set one key to cancel all open orders, as a quick way out if something goes wrong. You tested it first with simulated money.

Now count the time saved. Searching for a stock by name and opening its chart takes about 20 seconds. Five stocks on a watchlist take one glance. Over 10 lookups a day, that is 10 × 20 = 200 seconds, more than three minutes saved, and in a fast market three minutes matter.

A mistake in order entry shows the cost. If you had typed 1,000 shares instead of 100, at 50 dollars each, the position would be 50,000 dollars instead of 5,000: ten times larger than your plan, and probably more than your buying power allows. The platform would likely reject it. But a smaller slip, such as 150 instead of 100, would go through unnoticed.

| Tool | Helps with | Typical risk |
|---|---|---|
| Watchlist | Focus | Too many symbols, no focus |
| Charting platform | Analysis | Clutter and too many indicators |
| Order entry | Placing trades | Typing errors |
| Hotkey | Speed | Accidental orders |

Practice every tool in a simulated mode first, and keep your layout simple. Most platforms let you turn on a confirmation step. Many beginners keep it on until they trust their habits.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
A common slip is to switch platforms often and never learn one well. Another is setting up hotkeys before you have practiced, so that a slip sends a real order. Learn one platform, with confirmations on.

## Key points {#key-points}
- A trading platform is where you view, order and manage. A charting platform focuses on analysis.
- Order entry is where mistakes happen, so check every field.
- A watchlist narrows your attention. A hotkey trades safety for speed.
