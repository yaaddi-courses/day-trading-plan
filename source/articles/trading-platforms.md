# Trading platforms and their main tools

**Covers:** trading platform, trading-platform, order entry, watchlist, charting platform, hotkey

## The software between you and the market {#overview}
Everything a trader does, from watching prices to sending orders, happens through software. Choosing and learning that software is a real part of the job. A tool that is slow, or that you do not understand, can cost more than any bad idea.

## Trading platform {#trading-platform}
A trading platform is the software that you actually use to look at charts, place orders and manage your account. It may be your broker's own app or a separate program that connects to the broker. Platforms range from simple phone apps to desktop programs with many windows.

The hyphenated form, trading-platform, is the same term used before another noun, as in "a trading-platform feature".

## Order entry {#order-entry}
Order entry is the part of a platform where you type or click to build and send a buy or sell order. It usually has fields for the stock symbol, the number of shares, the order type and the price. A good order entry window shows exactly what will be sent, before you confirm. Many costly errors happen here, such as an extra zero in the share count.

## Watchlist {#watchlist}
A watchlist is a saved list of stocks that you follow closely, without holding a position in them yet. It shows their current prices and changes in one view. Traders build watchlists before the market opens: a short list of stocks that interest them today, so they can focus instead of searching in a hurry.

## Charting platform {#charting-platform}
A charting platform is software focused mainly on displaying and analyzing price charts. It may be separate from the platform where you place orders. It offers more drawing tools, more kinds of indicators and the ability to look back through history. Some traders use one program for charts and another for orders, though many platforms combine them.

## Hotkey {#hotkey}
A hotkey is a single keyboard shortcut, set up in advance, that sends a specific order at once. Desktop platforms offer it so that a trade does not need several clicks. For example, one key could sell your entire position at the current price. It is fast, and fast can be dangerous: a mistaken key press sends a real order.

## A worked example {#example}
A trader prepares a morning routine, step by step.

1. **Watchlist.** Before the open, she builds a list of five stocks that had news overnight. Instead of searching through thousands, she watches five.
2. **Charts.** She opens the charting platform and looks at each of the five. Two look interesting, three do not. The list is now two.
3. **Order entry.** When one reaches her planned level, she opens the order entry window. She types the symbol, 100 shares and a limit price. She checks the screen: symbol correct, quantity 100 and not 1,000, and price as planned. Only then does she confirm.
4. **Hotkey.** She has set one key to cancel all open orders, as a quick way out if something goes wrong. She tested it first with simulated money.

Count the time saved. Searching for a stock by name and opening its chart takes about 20 seconds. Five stocks on a watchlist take one glance. Over 10 lookups a day, that is more than three minutes saved, and in a fast market, three minutes matter.

A mistake in order entry shows the cost. If she had typed 1,000 shares instead of 100, at 50 dollars each, the position would be 50,000 dollars instead of 5,000: ten times larger than her plan, and probably more than her buying power allows. The platform would likely reject it, but a smaller slip, such as 150 instead of 100, would go through unnoticed.

| Tool | Helps with | Typical risk |
|---|---|---|
| Watchlist | Focus | Too many symbols, no focus |
| Charting platform | Analysis | Clutter and too many indicators |
| Order entry | Placing trades | Typing errors |
| Hotkey | Speed | Accidental orders |

Practice every tool in a simulated mode first, and keep the layout simple. Most platforms let you turn on a confirmation step, and beginners should keep it on until they trust their habits.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Switching platforms often, and never learning one well. Another mistake is setting up hotkeys before you have practiced, so that a slip sends a real order. Learn on one platform, with confirmations on.

## Key points {#key-points}
- A trading platform is where you view, order and manage. A charting platform is focused on analysis.
- Order entry is where mistakes happen, so check every field.
- A watchlist narrows your attention. A hotkey trades speed for risk.
