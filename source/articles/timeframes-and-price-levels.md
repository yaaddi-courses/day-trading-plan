# Timeframes, support, resistance and breaks

**Covers:** timeframe, chart interval, support, resistance, breakout, breakdown

## Choosing how closely to look {#overview}
The same price history can look calm or violent depending on how closely you zoom in. And on almost any chart, certain prices seem to attract attention again and again. In this article we look at the zoom setting first, and then at the price levels traders mark.

## Timeframe {#timeframe}
The timeframe is the length of time that one candlestick or bar on your chart stands for, such as 1 minute, 5 minutes or 1 day. On a 5-minute chart, every five minutes of trading make one candle. Short timeframes show detail, and noise with it. Long timeframes show the bigger picture.

## Chart interval {#chart-interval}
Chart interval is another name for the same setting. Many platforms label it "interval". Both words answer the same question: how much time does a single candlestick cover?

## Support {#support}
Support is a price where a falling stock has paused more than once, because buyers showed up there before. On the chart it looks like a floor. The more often the price has bounced from it, the more traders watch it. Support is not a guarantee. It is a place where buyers have been before.

## Resistance {#resistance}
Resistance is a price where a rising stock has stalled more than once, because sellers showed up there before. It looks like a ceiling. Like support, it is a memory of past behavior, and traders watch it because other traders do.

## Breakout {#breakout}
A breakout happens when the price pushes through resistance and keeps climbing instead of turning back down. Traders watch it because the old ceiling may become the new floor.

## Breakdown {#breakdown}
A breakdown is the reverse. The price pushes through support and keeps falling instead of bouncing, and the old floor may become the new ceiling.

## A worked example {#example}
A stock has moved like this over several days:

- Day 1: rose to 52.00, then fell back.
- Day 2: rose to 52.05, then fell back.
- Day 3: rose to 51.98, then fell back.
- On the way down each time, it stopped near 49.50 and rose again.

Let's draw the levels. Three tries failed to hold above 52.00, so the ceiling, the resistance, is near 52.00. Buyers stepped in near 49.50, so the floor, the support, is there. The stock moves in a channel 2.50 wide (52.00 − 49.50).

On day 4, the stock rises through 52.00 and closes at 52.80. That is a breakout. A trader who plans for it might write: "Enter if the price closes above 52.00. Exit if it falls back below 52.00." The risk is small and defined, because if the break fails, the exit sits only a little below the entry.

If instead the stock fell through 49.50 on day 4 and closed at 48.70, that would be a breakdown, with the same logic in reverse.

Many breaks fail. The price pokes above resistance for a few minutes and then drops back. Traders call that a false breakout. You can reduce the risk by waiting for the candle to close beyond the level, not only touch it, and by looking for strong activity on the break.

The timeframe changes the picture. Here is the same stock on two settings:

| Timeframe | What you see |
|---|---|
| 1-minute | Dozens of small ups and downs, many tiny "levels" |
| 1-day | The clear channel from 49.50 to 52.00 |

More traders respect levels on a long timeframe, so those levels are stronger than levels on a 1-minute chart. A common routine is to mark the levels on a daily chart first, then switch to a short timeframe to time the entry.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
A common slip is to treat a level as an exact number. Support at 49.50 may hold at 49.45 or fail at 49.55, so think of a level as a zone. Another slip is entering a breakout the moment the price touches the level, before you have seen whether it holds.

## Key points {#key-points}
- The timeframe, or chart interval, is the time each candlestick covers.
- Support is a floor where buyers have appeared. Resistance is a ceiling where sellers have.
- A breakout goes through resistance. A breakdown goes through support.
- Levels are zones, and levels on longer timeframes carry more weight.
