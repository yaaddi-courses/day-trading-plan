# Timeframes, support, resistance and breaks

**Covers:** timeframe, chart interval, support, resistance, breakout, breakdown

## Choosing how closely to look {#overview}
The same price history can look calm or violent depending on how closely you zoom. And on any chart, certain prices seem to attract attention again and again. This article covers the zoom setting and the price levels that traders mark.

## Timeframe {#timeframe}
The timeframe is the length of time that each candlestick or bar on a chart represents, such as 1 minute, 5 minutes or 1 day. A 5-minute chart makes one candle for every five minutes of trading. Short timeframes show detail and noise. Long ones show the bigger picture.

## Chart interval {#chart-interval}
Chart interval is another name for the timeframe setting. In the settings of many platforms, you will find it as "interval". Both words mean how much time each single candlestick covers.

## Support {#support}
Support is a price at which a falling stock has repeatedly paused, since buyers appeared there in the past. On the chart, it looks like a floor. The more often a price has bounced from it, the more traders watch it. Support is not a guarantee: it is a place where buyers have been before.

## Resistance {#resistance}
Resistance is a price at which a rising stock has repeatedly stalled, since sellers appeared there in the past. It looks like a ceiling. Like support, it is a memory of past behavior, and traders watch it because others do.

## Breakout {#breakout}
A breakout happens when the price pushes through resistance and keeps climbing past it, instead of turning back down. Traders watch it because the old ceiling may become the new floor.

## Breakdown {#breakdown}
A breakdown is the reverse: the price pushes through support and keeps falling past it, instead of bouncing. The old floor may become the new ceiling.

## A worked example {#example}
A stock has moved like this over several days:

- Day 1: rose to 52.00, then fell back.
- Day 2: rose to 52.05, then fell back.
- Day 3: rose to 51.98, then fell back.
- On the way down, each time, it stopped near 49.50 and rose again.

Draw the levels. The ceiling is near 52.00, because three tries failed to hold above it: resistance. The floor is near 49.50, where buyers stepped in: support. The stock is moving in a channel 2.50 wide (52.00 − 49.50).

On day 4, the stock rises through 52.00 and closes at 52.80. That is a breakout. A trader who plans for it might say: "Enter if the price closes above 52.00. Exit if it falls back below 52.00." The risk is small and defined: if the break fails, the exit is only a bit under the entry.

If instead on day 4, the stock falls through 49.50 and closes at 48.70, that is a breakdown, with the reverse logic.

Many breaks fail. A price pokes above resistance for a few minutes and then falls back. Traders call that a false breakout. Ways to reduce the risk: wait for the candle to close beyond the level, not just touch it, and look for strong activity on the break.

The timeframe changes the picture. Here is the same stock on two settings:

| Timeframe | What you see |
|---|---|
| 1-minute | Dozens of small ups and downs, many tiny "levels" |
| 1-day | The clear channel from 49.50 to 52.00 |

Levels on a long timeframe are respected by more traders, so they are stronger than levels on a 1-minute chart. A common routine is to mark levels on a daily chart first, then look at a short timeframe to time the entry.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Treating a level as an exact number. Support at 49.50 may hold at 49.45 or fail at 49.55. Think of levels as zones. Another mistake is entering a breakout the moment the price touches the level, without waiting to see whether it holds.

## Key points {#key-points}
- The timeframe, or chart interval, is the time each candlestick covers.
- Support is a floor where buyers have appeared. Resistance is a ceiling where sellers have.
- A breakout goes through resistance. A breakdown goes through support.
- Levels are zones, and levels on longer timeframes carry more weight.
