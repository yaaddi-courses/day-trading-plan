# What day trading is, and how it differs from holding

**Covers:** day trade, day trading, position trading, intraday, day-trade

## One question: how long do you hold? {#overview}
Think of two people who each buy shares. One plans to keep them for years because they believe in the company. The other plans to be out by the end of the afternoon. They may buy the very same stock. What separates them is how long they hold it, and that single choice changes the costs, the risks and how much attention each of them has to pay.

## Day trade {#day-trade}
A day trade is a purchase and a sale of the same stock inside one trading day. It also covers the reverse order, where you sell short first and buy the shares back later the same day. Both halves have to happen on that one day. If you buy on Monday and sell on Tuesday, that is not a day trade, even when the time between the two is short.

You will also see the hyphenated form, day-trade. It is the same idea used as a verb or an adjective, as in "a day-trade setup".

## Day trading {#day-trading}
Day trading means making day trades again and again. The trader tries to profit from small price moves and closes every position before the market closes, so nothing is held overnight. Some day traders make a few trades a day and some make dozens. The aim is not to own companies. It is to capture small price differences, over and over.

That style has a particular cost. Profit on each trade is small, so fees, mistakes and bad habits weigh heavily. Many beginners lose money, and a written plan is essential. That plan is what this course is about.

## Position trading {#position-trading}
Position trading is the opposite. You hold a stock for weeks, months or years, and you care about the company's long-term prospects more than the day-to-day price noise. You trade rarely, so costs stay low, and there is no daily pressure. The price you pay is time: your money is tied up for long stretches, and the position can fall a long way before it recovers.

## Intraday {#intraday}
Intraday means "within a single trading day". If a news site says a stock "fell 4% intraday", the fall happened during one day and not over several. Day traders work almost entirely with intraday moves, so you will meet this word often.

## A worked example {#example}
Two people each have 10,000 dollars, and each believes the same stock will rise.

**Maria, a position trader.** She buys 100 shares at 100 dollars on a Monday and holds them. Six months later the price is 112 dollars. She sells and earns 100 × (112 − 100) = 1,200 dollars, a gain of 12% on her 10,000. She made 2 trades in 6 months. On many days in between the price swung by several dollars, and she ignored it.

**Ken, a day trader.** On Tuesday he buys 100 shares at 100.00 at 10:05 and sells them at 100.60 at 10:50. The gain is 100 × 0.60 = 60 dollars. Later that day he makes two more trades. One loses 25 dollars and the other gains 40. By the close he holds nothing. His net for the day is 60 − 25 + 40 = 75 dollars, which is 0.75% of his 10,000.

| | Maria (position) | Ken (day) |
|---|---|---|
| Holding time | Months | Minutes to hours |
| Trades in a period | 2 in 6 months | 3 in one day |
| Overnight exposure | Yes, all the time | None |
| Needs | Patience | Attention and discipline |

Ken's 0.75% is a good day. Repeated over 20 trading days it would add up to about 15%, but he cannot count on that, because losing days are normal too. Each trade also carries costs, and one bad decision can erase several good days. That is why risk control, which comes later in the course, matters more than the entry.

Notice the vocabulary. All of Ken's trades are intraday, and each one is a day trade. If he had kept one position overnight, that part would no longer be a day trade.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
A common slip is to believe that day trading is investing at a faster pace. It is a different activity, with different skills and a high failure rate among newcomers. Another slip is holding a losing day trade overnight "to let it recover". That quietly turns it into an unplanned position trade, with risks you never chose.

## Key points {#key-points}
- A day trade opens and closes within one trading day. Day trading means doing that repeatedly.
- Position trading holds for weeks to years.
- Intraday means within one day.
- The difference is the holding time, and it changes the whole style of risk.
