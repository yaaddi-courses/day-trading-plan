# What day trading is, and how it differs from holding

**Covers:** day trade, day trading, position trading, intraday, day-trade

## One question: how long do you hold? {#overview}
People buy shares for very different reasons and for very different lengths of time. Some buy a company they believe in and keep it for years. Others try to profit from the small ups and downs of a single day. The difference is not what is bought, but how long it is held, and that one difference changes the costs, the risks and the amount of attention required.

## Day trade {#day-trade}
A day trade is buying and selling the same stock within one trading day, or selling short and buying it back in that same day. Both halves must happen on the same day. Buying a share on Monday and selling it on Tuesday is not a day trade, even if the time between is short. The term also covers the reverse order: selling first and buying back later in the day.

The hyphenated form, day-trade, is the same word used as a verb or adjective, as in "a day-trade setup".

## Day trading {#day-trading}
Day trading is the practice of making day trades repeatedly, aiming to profit from small price moves and closing every position before the market closes, so that nothing is held overnight. A day trader may make anything from a few trades to dozens in a day. The goal is not to own companies, but to capture small differences in price, over and over.

The approach has a specific cost: profits per trade are small, so costs, mistakes and bad habits matter a lot. Many beginners lose money, and a plan is essential. That is the subject of this course.

## Position trading {#position-trading}
Position trading is the opposite approach: holding a stock for weeks, months or years. A position trader cares about the company's long-term prospects and ignores the price noise of a single day. Trades are rare, so costs stay low, and there is no daily pressure. The cost is time: capital is tied up for long periods, and the position can fall a long way before it recovers.

## Intraday {#intraday}
Intraday means happening inside a single trading day, for example an intraday price move or an intraday trade. When someone says that a stock "fell 4% intraday", they mean during the day, not over several days. Day traders work almost entirely with intraday movements, which is why you will see this word often.

## A worked example {#example}
Two people each have 10,000 dollars and the same view that a certain stock will rise.

**Maria, a position trader.** She buys 100 shares at 100 dollars on a Monday and holds them. Six months later the price is 112 dollars. She sells and earns 100 × (112 − 100) = 1,200 dollars, a 12% gain on her 10,000. She made 2 trades in 6 months. In between, the price swung by several dollars on many days, which she ignored.

**Ken, a day trader.** On Tuesday, he buys 100 shares at 100.00 at 10:05 and sells them at 100.60 at 10:50. The gain is 100 × 0.60 = 60 dollars. He then makes two more trades that day: one loses 25 dollars, the other gains 40. By the closing time, he holds nothing. His net for the day is 60 − 25 + 40 = 75 dollars, which is 0.75% of his 10,000.

| | Maria (position) | Ken (day) |
|---|---|---|
| Holding time | Months | Minutes to hours |
| Trades in a period | 2 in 6 months | 3 in one day |
| Overnight exposure | Yes, all the time | None |
| Needs | Patience | Attention and discipline |

Ken's 0.75% is a good day. If he could repeat it for 20 trading days, the total would be about 15%, but he cannot count on it, since losing days are also normal. Each trade also has costs, and one bad decision can erase several good days. That is why risk control, covered later in the course, matters more than the entry.

A note on terms: all of Ken's trades are intraday, and each one is a day trade. If he had kept one position overnight, that part would no longer be a day trade.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Believing that day trading is just investing, faster. It is a different activity, with different skills and a high failure rate among newcomers. Another mistake is holding a losing day trade overnight "to let it recover". That turns it into an unplanned position trade, with risks you did not plan for.

## Key points {#key-points}
- A day trade opens and closes inside one trading day. Day trading is doing that repeatedly.
- Position trading holds for weeks to years.
- Intraday means within one day.
- The difference is the holding time, and it changes the whole style of risk.
