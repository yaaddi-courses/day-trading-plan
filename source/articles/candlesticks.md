# Reading a candlestick

**Covers:** candlestick, candle body, candle bodies, wick, bullish candle, bearish candle

## Four prices in one shape {#overview}
A line chart gives you one price for each moment, usually the last one. That hides a lot. During any five minutes, the price starts somewhere, wanders up and down, and ends somewhere else. A candlestick chart keeps four facts about each period: where the price started, where it ended, and the highest and lowest points it touched. Once you can read one candle, you can read a whole chart, so let's take a candle apart.

## Candlestick {#candlestick}
A candlestick is the shape drawn for one period of time, such as one minute or one day. It records the open, the close, the high and the low. It looks like a candle: a thick rectangle in the middle, with a thin line sticking out above and below. Every period gets its own candle, side by side from left to right.

## Candle body {#candle-body}
The thick rectangle is the candle body. It stretches from the opening price to the closing price. A tall body tells you the price travelled a long way between the start and the end of the period. A very short body tells you it ended close to where it began.

## Candle bodies {#candle-bodies}
You rarely read one body alone. Looking at several bodies in a row, you compare their sizes. A run of tall bodies pointing the same way shows steady pressure on the price. A run of small bodies shows hesitation: neither side is winning.

## Wick {#wick}
The thin lines above and below the body are wicks. The upper wick ends at the highest price of the period, and the lower wick ends at the lowest. A long wick means the price went far in one direction and was pushed back before the period closed.

## Wicks {#wicks}
Both wicks are read together with the body. Suppose a candle has a long upper wick and closes low. Buyers pushed the price up, then sellers pushed it back down. A long lower wick tells the opposite story: sellers pushed the price down, and buyers brought it back.

## Bullish candle {#bullish-candle}
A bullish candle is one that closed higher than it opened. Platforms usually color it green or white. The body runs from the open at the bottom up to the close at the top.

## Bearish candle {#bearish-candle}
A bearish candle is one that closed lower than it opened. It is usually red or black. The body runs from the open at the top down to the close at the bottom.

## A worked example {#example}
Here is one 5-minute period of a stock:

- Open: 50.00
- High: 50.60
- Low: 49.90
- Close: 50.40

Let's build the candle together.

1. The close (50.40) is above the open (50.00), so this is a bullish candle, drawn in green.
2. The body runs from 50.00 to 50.40, so its height is 0.40.
3. The upper wick runs from the top of the body (50.40) up to the high (50.60), a length of 0.20.
4. The lower wick runs from the bottom of the body (50.00) down to the low (49.90), a length of 0.10.

The whole range, low to high, is 50.60 − 49.90 = 0.70. The body covers 0.40 of it, about 57%. So most of the movement survived until the close.

Now the next period:

- Open: 50.40, High: 50.55, Low: 49.80, Close: 49.95.

The close is below the open, so this candle is bearish. The body spans 50.40 down to 49.95, a height of 0.45. The upper wick is 50.55 − 50.40 = 0.15, and the lower wick is 49.95 − 49.80 = 0.15. The range is 0.75. The story in words: after a rise, sellers took over and pushed the price below the level where the previous period had started.

| Feature | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Color or direction | Close above or below open |
| Body height | Strength of the move between open and close |
| Upper wick | How far buyers pushed before being driven back |
| Lower wick | How far sellers pushed before being driven back |

One candle is only a start, and it can mislead. A series of candles shows the contest between buyers and sellers. Colors also differ between platforms, so check the legend before you trust them.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
A common slip is to judge a candle by its color alone. A green candle with a very long upper wick means buyers gave back most of their gains before the close, and the color hides that. Look at the wicks as well. A second slip is forgetting the period: a one-minute candle and a one-day candle carry very different meanings.

## Key points {#key-points}
- A candlestick shows the open, close, high and low of one period.
- The body spans open to close, and the wicks reach the high and the low.
- Bullish means it closed above the open. Bearish means it closed below the open.
- Long wicks show prices that the market pushed back.
