# Reading a candlestick

**Covers:** candlestick, candle body, candle bodies, wick, bullish candle, bearish candle

## Four prices in one shape {#overview}
A line chart shows only one price per moment, usually the last. A candlestick chart shows four for each period: where the price started, where it ended, and the highest and lowest points it reached in between. Once you can read one candle, you can read a whole chart.

## Candlestick {#candlestick}
A candlestick is a single chart symbol showing a stock's open, close, high and low price for one time period. It looks like a candle with a body and thin lines, called wicks, above and below. Each period, such as one minute or one day, gets one candlestick.

## Candle body {#candle-body}
The candle body is the thick rectangular part of a candlestick. It shows the distance between the opening price and the closing price. A tall body means the price moved a lot between the start and the end of the period. A very short body means it ended close to where it began.

## Candle bodies {#candle-bodies}
Candle bodies, in the plural, are compared with each other across a chart. A run of tall bodies in the same direction shows steady pressure on the price. Small bodies, in a row, show hesitation.

## Wick {#wick}
The wick is the thin line above or below the body. It shows the highest and lowest prices reached during the period. The upper wick ends at the high, the lower wick at the low. A long wick means that the price went far in one direction and was pushed back before the period ended.

## Wicks {#wicks}
Wicks are read together with the body. A long upper wick on a candle that ended low shows that buyers pushed the price up, and sellers pushed it back. A long lower wick shows the reverse.

## Bullish candle {#bullish-candle}
A bullish candle is a candlestick where the closing price ended higher than the opening price. It is usually colored green or white. The body runs from the open at the bottom to the close at the top.

## Bearish candle {#bearish-candle}
A bearish candle is a candlestick where the closing price ended lower than the opening price. It is usually colored red or black. The body runs from the open at the top down to the close.

## A worked example {#example}
A stock during one 5-minute period:

- Open: 50.00
- High: 50.60
- Low: 49.90
- Close: 50.40

Build the candle:

1. The close (50.40) is above the open (50.00), so the candle is bullish, and colored green.
2. The body runs from 50.00 to 50.40: a height of 0.40.
3. The upper wick runs from the top of the body (50.40) up to the high (50.60): a length of 0.20.
4. The lower wick runs from the bottom of the body (50.00) down to the low (49.90): a length of 0.10.

The total range from low to high is 50.60 − 49.90 = 0.70. The body takes 0.40 of that, which is about 57%. So most of the move held.

Next period:

- Open: 50.40, High: 50.55, Low: 49.80, Close: 49.95.

The close is below the open, so the candle is bearish. The body spans 50.40 to 49.95, which is 0.45. The upper wick is 50.55 − 50.40 = 0.15, and the lower wick is 49.95 − 49.80 = 0.15. The range is 0.75. The story: after rising, sellers took control and pushed the price below where the previous period began.

| Feature | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Color or direction | Close above or below open |
| Body height | Strength of the move between open and close |
| Upper wick | How far buyers pushed before being driven back |
| Lower wick | How far sellers pushed before being driven back |

Reading a single candle is only a start. One candle can be misleading, but a series of them shows the contest between buyers and sellers. The color scheme can differ between platforms, so check the legend before trusting the colors.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Judging the candle by its color alone. A green candle with a very long upper wick means that buyers lost most of their gains before the close. Look at the wicks. Another mistake is forgetting that the period decides the meaning: a one-minute candle and a one-day candle are very different things.

## Key points {#key-points}
- A candlestick shows open, close, high and low for one period.
- The body spans open to close. The wicks reach the high and low.
- Bullish: closed above the open. Bearish: closed below the open.
- Long wicks show rejected prices.
